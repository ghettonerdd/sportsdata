Plain-English definitions for every stat used on this site

BETTING BASICS

MoneylineA bet on who wins the game outright — no point spread involved. A negative number (e.g. -175) shows what you'd need to bet to win $100. A positive number (e.g. +145) shows what a $100 bet would win.
SpreadPoints added to the underdog (or subtracted from the favorite) to make the bet closer to a coin flip. The favorite has to win by more than the spread to "cover"; the underdog covers by losing by less than the spread or winning outright.
Total (Over/Under)A bet on the combined final score of both teams — will it go over or under the posted number?
Team TotalSame idea as the Total, but just for one team's own points, not the combined score.
Implied Win ProbabilityThe win chance a moneyline is suggesting, converted into a percentage. A -175 favorite implies roughly a 64% chance to win, for example.
Key NumbersThe final margins that come up most often in NFL games — 3 and 7 points, above all. Spreads that sit right on or near these numbers matter more than others.
Public Betting %The share of bets (or dollars) being placed on each side. When the betting money is lopsided but the line hasn't moved much, that can be a signal "sharp" money is on the other side.
SU (Straight-Up) RecordPlain win-loss record — did the team win the game, regardless of the spread?
ATS (Against The Spread) RecordWin-loss record based on covering the spread, not just winning the game. A team can win SU but lose ATS if they didn't win by enough.

EFFICIENCY METRICS

EPA (Expected Points Added)Measures how much a single play changes a team's expected points, based on the down, distance, and field position it happened in. A play with positive EPA helped the offense; negative EPA helped the defense. "EPA per play" is just the average across all plays — higher is better for an offense, and lower (more negative) is better for a defense.
EPA DifferentialA team's offensive EPA/play minus its defensive EPA/play allowed — a quick one-number summary of overall team strength.
DVOA (Defense-adjusted Value Over Average)A rating that compares every single play to the league-average result for that same situation, then adjusts for the strength of the opponent faced. It's published by FTN Fantasy / Football Outsiders, not freely available via API. Higher DVOA is better on offense; more negative DVOA is better on defense.
Success RateThe percentage of plays that counted as "successful" for the down: roughly gaining 40% of the needed yards on 1st down, 60% on 2nd down, and 100% on 3rd or 4th down. It measures consistency rather than big, game-breaking plays.
Success Rate AllowedThe same idea, from the defense's side — the percentage of opponent plays that were "successful" against them. Lower is better defense.
ANY/A (Adjusted Net Yards per Attempt)A passing-efficiency stat that folds in yards, touchdowns, interceptions, and sacks into one number — a fuller picture of passing performance than plain yards per attempt.
ANY/A DifferentialA team's own ANY/A on offense minus the ANY/A it allows on defense.

SCORING & RECORD METRICS

Point DifferentialTotal points scored minus total points allowed over the season — a simple, reliable measure of how dominant a team has been.
Pythagorean WinsAn expected win total calculated from points scored and allowed, rather than actual game outcomes. Teams that have won more games than their Pythagorean number suggests are often considered "due" for regression, and vice versa.
Turnover DifferentialTurnovers gained (interceptions + fumble recoveries) minus turnovers lost. One of the stats most closely tied to winning.
3rd Down %The percentage of third downs converted into a first down (offense) or allowed to be converted (defense).
Red Zone TD %The percentage of trips inside the opponent's 20-yard line that end in a touchdown, rather than a field goal or turnover.
Time of PossessionHow long a team's offense controls the ball over the course of a game. More time doesn't always mean more points, but it does mean fewer possessions for the other team.
Yards Per PlayA basic efficiency stat — total yards divided by total plays. Simpler than EPA or success rate, and less predictive, but still a useful sanity check.
Sack Rate / Pressure RateHow often a quarterback is sacked or pressured (bad for the offense, good for the defense) relative to how often they drop back to pass.
Penalty Yards DifferentialThe net penalty yardage between the two teams — positive means the team has benefited from fewer/cheaper penalties than its opponent.

A few of these (DVOA especially) aren't available through a free live data feed — those need to be checked and entered by hand each week from a source like FTN Fantasy.
